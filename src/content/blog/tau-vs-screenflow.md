ScreenFlow is one of the most polished screen recorders on macOS. If you've looked into recording your screen on a Mac, you've likely come across it. It's well-designed, capable, and widely used for course creation and tutorials.

But if timelapse is what you're after, ScreenFlow is the wrong tool. Here's why.

## What ScreenFlow is built for

ScreenFlow is a screen recorder and video editor. Its core workflow is:

1. Record your screen (at full frame rate)
2. Edit the recording in a full timeline editor
3. Add callouts, annotations, zoom effects, and transitions
4. Export for YouTube, Vimeo, or other platforms

This is an excellent workflow for tutorials, software demos, and course content. ScreenFlow does it very well.

## Where it falls short for timelapse

**No frame interval recording.** ScreenFlow records at full frame rate — 30fps or 60fps, whatever you set. A timelapse requires capturing one frame every N seconds, then playing those frames back at normal speed. ScreenFlow doesn't do this. To make a timelapse from a ScreenFlow recording, you'd record hours of full-rate video, then manually speed it up in the editor. That's a large raw file, manual post-processing, and not the same as true timelapse capture.

**macOS only.** ScreenFlow doesn't run on Windows.

**Overkill for timelapse.** ScreenFlow's timeline editor, callouts, and effects are powerful — but if all you want is to trim a clip and export it, you're paying for a lot of features you won't use.

**$149.** ScreenFlow starts at $149 for a perpetual license. There's also a subscription option. For timelapse use, this is significant pricing for a tool that doesn't natively support timelapse capture.

**No GIF or WEBM export.** ScreenFlow exports to MOV and MP4. GIF and WEBM require workarounds or third-party tools.

## What Tau does differently

Tau is built specifically for timelapse. Frame interval capture is the core of the product — not an afterthought.

Set your interval (1 frame every 5 seconds, every 10 seconds, whatever fits your session), start recording, and Tau runs quietly in the background. When you're done:

- Trim start and end points in the built-in editor
- Add a timer overlay (stopwatch, clock, or countdown) with one click
- Attach background audio if you want it
- Export to MP4, GIF, or WEBM — no additional tools

Tau works on both macOS and Windows. It costs €9.99 for a Solo license (1 device) or €17.99 for Pro (3 devices). One-time payment.

## Which should you use?

**Use ScreenFlow if:** you're creating tutorials, courses, or software demos that need a full timeline editor, zoom effects, and callout annotations. It's the right tool for that.

**Use Tau if:** you want to capture your work as a timelapse — a coding session, a design process, an art piece — and export something polished without the overhead of a full video editor.

They're genuinely built for different things. The overlap is that both record your screen. The difference is everything that happens after.
